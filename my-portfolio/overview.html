M.S. in Applied Data Science - Syracuse University

Overview

Program Learning Outcomes

The M.S. in Applied Data Science at Syracuse University is an interdisciplinary program designed to prepare practitioners who can generate actionable insights from data. Throughout my studies, I have worked to master the following program learning outcomes:

1
Data Infrastructure: Collect, store, and access data by identifying and leveraging applicable technologies.
2
Actionable Insight: Create actionable insight across a range of contexts (societal, business, political) using data and the full data science life cycle.
3
Visualization & Modeling: Apply visualization and predictive models to help generate actionable insight.
4
Programming: Use programming languages such as R and Python to support the generation of actionable insight.
5
Communication: Communicate insights gained via visualization and analytics to a broad range of audiences (including project sponsors and technical team leads).
6
Ethics: Apply ethics in the development, use and evaluation of data and predictive models (fairness, bias, transparency, privacy).

High-Level Project Descriptions

1
Bitcoin Flash-Crash Prediction (Price + Reddit Sentiment): Built a binary sequence model to detect flash-crash minutes by combining minute-level Bitcoin market behavior with aligned Reddit sentiment features. The project focuses on interpretable risk monitoring for rare-event market moves.
2
Drowsiness Detection from Infrared Eye Images: Developed a CNN pipeline that classifies infrared eye crops as awake vs sleepy. The system demonstrates how computer vision can support early driver-alert prototypes when paired with real-world validation.
3
MediExplain - RAG and Modular AI Assistants: Created a Streamlit prototype that translates complex medical text into plain-language explanations, supports retrieval-grounded responses (RAG), and orchestrates modular assistant workflows for synthetic clinical demos.

Project Descriptions

1
Bitcoin Flash-Crash Prediction (Price + Reddit Sentiment) Course / Context: IST-707 Applied Machine Learning.
Goals: Implement core machine-learning methods and build a predictive model that detects potential flash-crash behavior in cryptocurrency markets by combining price action and sentiment context.
Technologies Used: Python, PyTorch, scikit-learn, pandas, NumPy, matplotlib, seaborn, VADER sentiment, and FinBERT-based sentiment scoring.
Actionable Insight Generated: The model showed that combining market microstructure with Reddit sentiment can improve risk signaling for rare downside events. Threshold tuning (for example 0.3 vs 0.4) provided a practical precision-recall tradeoff that can support monitoring decisions rather than one-size-fits-all alerts.
My Specific Contribution: I owned the project end-to-end: feature engineering, sentiment-data integration, model training, evaluation design, threshold analysis, and final interpretation/reporting.
Program Learning Outcomes Demonstrated: 2 (Actionable Insight), 3 (Visualization & Modeling), 4 (Programming), 5 (Communication), and 6 (Ethics via careful interpretation of imbalanced predictions and model limits).
2
Drowsiness Detection from Infrared Eye Images Course / Context: IST 691 Deep Learning in Practice.
Goals: Build a deep-learning computer-vision pipeline that classifies infrared eye-region images into awake vs sleepy classes, forming a baseline for driver drowsiness alert systems.
Technologies Used: TensorFlow/Keras, Python, OpenCV, NumPy, pandas, scikit-learn, matplotlib, and dlib for eye-region processing and model evaluation workflows.
Actionable Insight Generated: A compact CNN with disciplined preprocessing and augmentation can deliver strong classification performance and support practical early-warning prototypes, while also highlighting where real-world validation (lighting, angle, domain shift) is needed before deployment.
My Specific Contribution: I completed dataset exploration, preprocessing pipeline setup, CNN design and training experiments, metrics analysis (precision/recall/F1, ROC/AUC), and final technical documentation.
Program Learning Outcomes Demonstrated: 1 (Data Infrastructure), 2 (Actionable Insight), 3 (Visualization & Modeling), 4 (Programming), and 5 (Communication).
3
MediExplain - RAG and Modular AI Assistants Course / Context: IST 688 Building HC-AI Apps.
Goals: Build a human-centered AI app that translates complex medical language into plain-language explanations, grounds outputs using retrieval-augmented generation (RAG), and maintains explicit safety boundaries.
Technologies Used: Python, Streamlit, OpenAI API, ChromaDB, embedding/retrieval pipelines, BeautifulSoup/lxml parsing, and modular assistant orchestration patterns.
Actionable Insight Generated: A modular, retrieval-grounded architecture improves transparency and user trust by separating retrieval, reasoning, and explanation steps, making outputs easier to validate in healthcare education contexts.
My Specific Contribution: I designed the application flow, implemented ingestion/retrieval components, integrated modular assistants, and embedded safety/disclaimer framing for responsible use.
Program Learning Outcomes Demonstrated: 1 (Data Infrastructure), 2 (Actionable Insight), 4 (Programming), 5 (Communication), and 6 (Ethics).

Reflections on My Journey

April 2026
Blog Post Title: A Letter to My Future Self: Reflecting on Applied Data Science at Syracuse

Dear Future Me,

As I complete my M.S. in Applied Data Science at Syracuse University, I can clearly see how much this program changed the way I think, build, and communicate. I came in wanting to become stronger technically, but I am leaving with something bigger: an end-to-end data science mindset that starts with problem framing and ends with practical decisions.

What I Expected to Learn

When I started the program, I expected to sharpen my machine learning skills, improve my modeling accuracy, and become faster with tools like Python, SQL, and visualization frameworks. I also expected structured exposure to industry-style workflows so I could confidently move from class projects to real-world solutions.

What I did not fully expect was how much emphasis would be placed on ambiguity, stakeholder context, and communication. I thought data science was mostly about building models; I learned that it is about solving the right problem first, then selecting methods that are useful, explainable, and responsible.

What I Actually Learned

The strongest outcome of this program is that I now approach projects with a full lifecycle mindset: define the problem, audit the data, engineer features, validate methods, communicate findings, and document limitations. Each learning outcome became practical through repeated project cycles.

Outcome 1 - Data Infrastructure: I learned how to structure messy, real datasets into reliable pipelines. This includes ingestion strategy, schema consistency, preprocessing discipline, and storage choices that support analysis and model reproducibility.

Outcome 2 - Actionable Insight: I learned to move from "what happened" to "what should we do next." Instead of reporting metrics in isolation, I now tie findings to decisions, tradeoffs, and risk-management actions.

Outcome 3 - Visualization and Modeling: I learned that evaluation quality matters more than headline accuracy. Precision, recall, confusion matrices, ROC/AUC, and threshold tuning became essential tools for trustworthy model interpretation.

Outcome 4 - Programming: Across Python and R workflows, I became more disciplined in writing modular, reproducible code. I now think in terms of reusable components, not one-off notebooks.

Outcome 5 - Communication: I improved my ability to explain technical outputs to mixed audiences. I now frame results with context, recommendation, and limitation, so the work supports decision-making rather than staying academic.

Outcome 6 - Ethics: The program reinforced fairness, transparency, and responsible use. I now treat bias risk, uncertainty, and model boundaries as central project deliverables, not optional add-ons.

The Projects That Shaped Me

Three projects from my portfolio best represent how I developed as an applied data scientist.

Project 1 - Bitcoin Flash-Crash Prediction (Price + Reddit Sentiment): This project taught me how to frame a rare-event prediction problem, align market and sentiment features, and evaluate beyond accuracy. I worked on threshold calibration, class-imbalance interpretation, and practical alert tradeoffs. It strengthened my confidence in machine learning evaluation under realistic constraints.

Project 2 - Deep Learning Drowsiness Detection Using Infrared Images: This project helped me operationalize computer vision workflows from preprocessing to CNN evaluation. I learned how data quality, augmentation, and validation design affect performance reliability. It also reinforced that model performance must be interpreted in deployment context, not just in static scores.

Project 3 - MediExplain (RAG and Modular AI Assistants): This project became the clearest example of human-centered AI design in my portfolio. I built a retrieval-grounded workflow to explain medical content in plain language while preserving safety framing. It strengthened my skills in retrieval design, modular architecture, and responsible communication for sensitive domains.

Beyond the Classroom

My work outside class at ProHealth Home Health & Hospice and NEXIS gave me valuable practical exposure. These experiences reinforced the realities of applied data science: incomplete data, evolving business requirements, tight timelines, and the need to communicate tradeoffs clearly. They helped me bridge classroom rigor with production-minded execution.

My Favorite Class

My favorite class was IST 688 - Building HC-AI Apps. It stood out because it combined technical implementation with human-centered design and ethical responsibility. The course pushed me to design AI systems that are not only functional, but also understandable, safe, and useful for real users. It directly shaped how I approached MediExplain and strengthened my confidence in building practical AI applications with clear communication and guardrails.

The Best Parts and Biggest Surprises

The best part of the program was the shift from assignment-based thinking to solution-based thinking. Over time, I stopped focusing only on "building a model" and started focusing on "delivering a useful, trusted outcome." That change in mindset is the biggest value I am taking forward.

The biggest surprises were how much impact communication has, how much project quality depends on data preparation, and how critical evaluation discipline is for trustworthy deployment. I also gained much more confidence than I expected: I now feel prepared to scope, build, and present end-to-end data science work in professional environments.

Looking Forward

Completing this program has been a meaningful milestone. I leave with stronger technical depth, better reasoning under ambiguity, and clearer communication habits. More importantly, I now have a repeatable process for turning complex data problems into practical, actionable outcomes.

I am proud of the growth this journey represents, and I am excited to apply these skills in real settings where data, AI, and human-centered design intersect. The goal ahead is simple: keep building solutions that are technically strong, ethically grounded, and genuinely useful.

With gratitude and anticipation,
Hari Ram Selvaraj
Syracuse University, Class of 2026
